How it works.

The low-voltage controller subsystem of the DIY TIG welding positioner: the GUITION JC4880P443C ESP32-P4 touch board, its speed pot, direction switch, foot pedal (switch plus ADS1115 pot), conditioned E-STOP input and the DM542T signal terminal. Motor power, the stepper, gearbox and the grounded metal enclosure are outside this package.

1

Set the speed

Turn the pot or tap the touch screen. The rotator holds the surface speed you chose.

2

Start with your foot

Press the pedal to start and let go to stop, so both hands stay on the torch and filler.

3

Stop through the driver

The E-STOP input and the driver alarm make the firmware stop pulses and inhibit ENA, and need a reset before it runs again. This path goes through the controller and does not disconnect motor power.

Low-voltage control wiring for the ESP32-P4 TIG welding rotator.

One pin map

The wiring guide and the firmware pin header come from the same file, so GPIO numbers cannot drift apart.

Driver alarm wired

The DM542T alarm output is read back, so a driver that reports a fault (over-current, over-voltage) stops the job. It cannot see a stall without an encoder.

Pedal on its own ADC

An ADS1115 on the touch I2C bus reads the pedal pot, leaving the board ADC for the panel pot.

Built for HF

Everything lives in one grounded metal enclosure with the driver and PSU, which TIG HF start requires.

What's inside.

7 parts. Total: about 20 USD, estimated from typical prices, not sourced.

DM542T signal terminal

The stepper driver that turns step pulses into motor current. Only its 8-way signal terminal is wired from the controller; motor and 24-36 V power are separate.
1 xowned

Speed pot

The ordinary round panel potentiometer: it is fixed in a hole with a nut, a knob goes on the shaft, and three wires are soldered to its lugs. The value (10 k linear is usual) is set per project.
1 xabout 1.50 USD, estimated

Direction switch CW/CCW

A small panel toggle switch with a metal lever and two positions; it connects the middle lug to one of the two outer lugs, so it works as an on/off or a changeover switch.
1 xabout 1 USD, estimated

E-STOP interface input (3V3 / SIG / GND)

The blue three-way PCB screw terminal: an outside wire is clamped under each screw, so it can be taken off again with a screwdriver. Blocks slide together side by side for longer rows.
1 xabout 0.40 USD, estimated

Foot pedal socket

A small round metal socket with four pins that is fixed in a hole in the case with a nut; a cable with the matching screw-on plug connects a sensor or motor outside the case.
1 xabout 2 USD, estimated

Pedal ADC (ADS1115)

1 xabout 15 USD, estimated

ESP32-P4 touch board (JP1)

The controller: a 4.3 inch touch screen in a metal frame with the ESP32-P4 board on its back. Every wire in this build lands on its 2x13 JP1 header near the right edge.

Questions, answered.

Can I print a plastic case for this?

No. TIG HF start needs the controller, driver and PSU in one grounded metal enclosure. This package leaves the enclosure out on purpose.

Is the E-STOP a safety circuit?

No. It is a supervised input on GPIO34 that the firmware acts on through ENA. It is not independent of the controller and does not cut motor power. Verify pressed, released, broken wire and power loss against docs/estop_timing.md before use.

Has this wiring been built?

The firmware and pin map have run on the owner's machine (v2.3.1). This generated guide has not been followed by anyone yet; check each step against HARDWARE_SETUP.md.

Can the ESP32-P4 drive the DM542T directly?

Only if your driver's input specification allows 3.3 V drive. The checker flags it as marginal; use a level interface if the manual asks for 5 V signals.
